All four lineups are built under that week's own salaries using the same engine described below — "Recommended", "Sim Median", and "Sim Ceiling" are exactly what the tool would have suggested before kickoff under each of /nfl/lineup's three optimizer objectives, from data available at the time; "Best possible" is true hindsight, the ceiling every prediction is measured against. Sim Median/Sim Ceiling reuse whatever the game simulator's daily background run last computed before that week rolled over — very close to pre-game, but not quite as strictly leakage-gated as Recommended's own before-kickoff cutoff; a week the simulator never got to run for falls back to matching Recommended exactly. Roster and position always come from each team's CURRENT roster, not a historical snapshot, since no historical roster lookback exists in this app's data sources — a player since traded or cut can't appear here even if they actually played that week, and a since-signed player can appear even if they weren't on the roster yet. Injury-status tracking only started recently, so older weeks rarely exclude anyone as confirmed-out. A ✓ next to a player in Recommended/Sim Median/Sim Ceiling means they also appear in that same week's Best Possible lineup — a real hindsight-correct pick, not just a plausible one. The "Top by actual points, per position" tables rank every player in that week's pool (not just the 9 in any one lineup) by what they actually scored, split by position rather than one combined list — QBs score highest on average but a lineup only ever rosters one, so a flat ranking would be mostly QBs and say little about the RB/WR/TE/DST picks. Badges show which of our three lineups (if any) picked each player — useful for seeing not just whether a player made a lineup, but how far off the real positional ceiling they were.
